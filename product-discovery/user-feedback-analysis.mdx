## Use this prompt

Analyse the customer/user feedback provided below to help me understand what users are saying, experiencing, and asking for.

Product/company: [product or company]

Target users/customers: [brief description]

Feedback sources: [interviews / surveys / support tickets / reviews / other]

Time period: [if relevant]

Research intent or questions: [what I am trying to learn from this feedback]

Feedback/data: [PASTE OR ATTACH THE FEEDBACK HERE]

Analyse the feedback in relation to the stated research intent. Look across responses rather than treating each response as an isolated data point, and identify the patterns, insights, and signals that are most relevant.

Do not limit the analysis to positive and negative sentiment. Surface whatever findings are supported by the feedback, including recurring needs, pain points, unmet expectations, requests, behaviours, motivations, objections, confusion, feature feedback, product usage patterns, emerging themes, or other meaningful signals.

Distinguish recurring patterns from one-off comments or individual preferences. Where useful, identify meaningful differences between user segments, use cases, or other groups. Preserve contradictory or divergent feedback rather than forcing it into a single conclusion.

Base findings on the provided feedback and clearly distinguish between what users explicitly said or reported, patterns inferred from multiple responses, and hypotheses or interpretations that require further validation. Do not invent motivations, needs, explanations, or patterns that are not supported by the data.

Return:

**Executive summary**: The most important findings, prioritised by relevance to the research intent.
**Key themes**: The major patterns identified and a concise explanation of each.
**Evidence**: Representative examples or excerpts supporting each important theme, with frequency or consistency indicated where the data allows.
**Notable differences or contradictions**: Meaningful variation across users, segments, use cases, or responses.
**Unexpected findings**: Relevant patterns or signals that were not explicitly part of the research intent but appear important.
**Implications**: What the findings could mean for the product, user experience, or further research. Clearly distinguish evidence from interpretation.
**Open questions**: Important questions raised by the feedback that the available data does not answer.

Prioritise evidence-backed, decision-useful insights over exhaustive summarisation. Do not force the feedback into predetermined categories or manufacture patterns.
