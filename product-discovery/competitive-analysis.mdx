# Conduct Competitive Analysis

> Use this prompt to conduct competitive analysis for your product or company

## Use this prompt

Conduct a competitive analysis for [PRODUCT/COMPANY]

Product: [brief description]

Target users/customers: [who the product is for]

Problem it solves: [core problem]

Market/category: [market or category]

Geographic market: [if relevant]

Known competitors: [competitors, if any]

Other relevant context: [optional]

Use the information provided as the basis for the analysis. If the competitive context is incomplete, research the market where possible to identify additional direct, indirect, or substitute competitors that are relevant to the product.

Analyse every competitor provided by me. Do not remove, replace, or reclassify a provided competitor based on your own assessment. If you believe a provided competitor is not a conventional direct competitor, retain it in the analysis and explain its competitive relevance or relationship separately.

Assess the dimensions of competition that are most meaningful for this market rather than applying a generic feature checklist. Consider factors such as product capabilities, target users, key use cases, user experience, pricing, positioning, distribution, integrations, and other category-specific factors where relevant.

Focus on differences that are meaningful to customers and strategically relevant. Do not assume that having more features, a lower price, or a broader offering makes one product better.

For important findings, distinguish between what is supported by evidence, what is an inference, and what remains unknown. Do not present an inference as an established fact, and do not fill evidence gaps with assumptions.

Return:

**Competitive landscape**: Identify the most relevant competitors, including all competitors provided, and briefly explain why each matters.
**Comparison**: Create a concise comparison of the product and competitors across the dimensions that are most strategically important to this market.
**Key insights**: Identify the most important competitive patterns, strengths, weaknesses, differentiators, gaps, and areas of meaningful similarity.
**Strategic implications**: Explain what the findings mean for the product. Distinguish between opportunities worth pursuing and competitive gaps that are not strategically important to address.
**Recommendations**: Provide 3–5 specific actions the product team should consider based on the analysis.
**Open questions**: Identify important unknowns or assumptions that should be validated before making a significant product decision.

Prioritise decision-useful insight over comprehensiveness. Do not pad the analysis with generic observations or recommendations. If the available evidence is insufficient to support a conclusion, say so clearly rather than filling the gap with an assumption.
