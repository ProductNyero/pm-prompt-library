# Use this prompt

Write a product demo script based on the product information and materials provided below.

Product / feature being demonstrated: [product, feature, or experience]

PRD or product documentation: [paste or attach]

Demo audience: [customer, prospect, investor, internal audience, etc.]

Industry / market: [industry and/or market, if relevant]

Desired runtime: [e.g. 5 minutes, 10 minutes, long-form]

Features or capabilities to spotlight: [list any features that deserve more attention]

Desired language and tone: [e.g. technical, conversational, executive, educational]

Reference video, site, or other example: [URL or material, if available]

Use the PRD and other product information as the source of truth for what the product does, who it is for, and the problems it addresses. Build the narrative around what will be most relevant and compelling to the specified audience.

If a reference video, site, or other example is provided, study it for useful characteristics such as structure, pacing, sequencing, level of detail, presentation style, and how it communicates value. Use those characteristics as inspiration, but do not imitate, reproduce, or closely mirror its wording, narrative, structure, or creative treatment. The resulting script should be original and appropriate to the product and audience provided.

Give greater emphasis to any features the user has specifically identified, while still creating a coherent demonstration rather than treating the script as a list of features. Prioritise the parts of the product that best help the audience understand its value and how it works.

Adapt the depth, terminology, examples, pacing, and level of technical detail to the specified audience, industry, market, and tone. Stay within the requested runtime and allocate time proportionally to the importance of each part of the demonstration.

Do not invent product capabilities, workflows, claims, customer outcomes, or technical details that are not supported by the provided material. Where the available information is insufficient to write an accurate section, flag the gap rather than filling it with assumptions.

Write the script so that it can be used directly to deliver the demo, including what should be said and, where useful, brief cues for what should be shown on screen.

The final script should have a clear narrative, natural transitions, appropriate pacing, and a strong connection between what is being demonstrated and why it matters to the intended audience. It should feel like an original demo designed for this product, not a generic product-demo template.
