## Use this prompt 

Develop a product requirements document for the product or feature described below.

Product / feature: [name or description]

Context and information: [product brief, problem context, customer information, research, business context, notes, existing documentation, or other relevant information]

Target users / customers: [if known]

Business objective: [if known]

Additional requirements or constraints: [if known]

Use the information provided to develop the PRD. Preserve the distinction between what is explicitly known, what can reasonably be inferred, and what remains unknown.

The PRD should cover, where relevant:

**Problem statement**
**Business objectives**
**User stories**
**Design requirements**
**Functional requirements**
**Expected behaviour / user journey**
**Features**
**Edge cases**
**Metrics to track**

Translate the available information into specific, actionable requirements. User stories should reflect the needs or goals of the identified users. Design and functional requirements should describe what the experience or product needs to support, while expected behaviour should make the intended user journey and system response clear.

Include features when they are relevant to the product or feature being documented. Do not manufacture features simply to make the PRD appear complete.

Do not invent user needs, business objectives, research findings, product capabilities, requirements, metrics, or other facts that are not supported by the information provided. When a necessary piece of information is missing, identify it as an open question or assumption rather than presenting it as fact.

Where the information provided contains ambiguity or conflicting requirements, surface the issue clearly rather than silently choosing an interpretation.

Keep the requirements specific enough to guide product decisions and subsequent design and development work, while avoiding implementation details that have not been established as requirements.

Return the completed PRD in a logical order, with enough detail to make the intended product behaviour and requirements unambiguous.
